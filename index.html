Why I started

AI can give an impressive answer. Can it do the work?

My first engineering job started in 2007. I spent most of the years since on telephony and high-availability systems. In that work, a call that guesses its route is not a small bug, it's an outage. That left me with little patience for software that is only approximately right.

Sources: LinkedIn profile · LinkedIn post, 16 Jan 2026

I joined BITLogix as a senior software engineer in 2009 and grew from there through architecture and R&D to EVP Engineering, which I left on 30 October 2026. Along the way I founded TechHatch in 2021 to mentor graduates through real projects. Since 2023 I've also led MisGenie as CEO.

Source: LinkedIn experience

When AI agents arrived, I saw the old problem again: confident output that is sometimes made up. In my own tests, a leading model invented a date in 3 of 39 runs. Checking the required fields in code before any tool ran brought that to zero. In 50 code edits I tracked, 16 needed no model at all.

Sources: X, 5 Nov 2026 · X, 6 Nov 2026

In November 2026 I left my job to work on that full time. I start from the business problem, not the model. The work has to fit how a company already runs, respect who is allowed to do what, and keep a person accountable.

Source: LinkedIn About

What I'm building

MAGSpace

MAGSpace is where I build products that let AI agents do business work while people stay in charge. An agent takes only an action it's allowed to take, asks when a detail is missing, and stops when nothing fits. Changes such as refunds wait for a person to confirm. Each product site says plainly whether it's live, in beta or in early access.

Research · September 2026

Exact signals in whole numbers

Medical images and audio start as whole numbers: pixels and samples. I wanted to know whether they could be represented without floating-point rounding and still be recovered exactly. In September 2026 I published five preprints on Zenodo that prove when this kind of representation over binary-power rings can be uniquely recovered, and the verification code is public.

  1. Quantinion Simplex Cubic Decomposition over 2-Power Rings: Global Identifiability from Binary Contractions and Exact Bit LiftingDOI 10.5281/zenodo.22850767
  2. Exact Free Capacity of Symmetric Rank-One Cubic Dictionaries over Dyadic RingsDOI 10.5281/zenodo.22850810
  3. An Eight-State Operator and Transpose Geometry over Dyadic Rings: Trace-Zero Modules, Mixed-Radix Addressing, and Tensor-Lift KernelsDOI 10.5281/zenodo.22850830
  4. Dimension-Free Identifiability of (n+2) Symmetric Cubics over Dyadic RingsDOI 10.5281/zenodo.22850834
  5. Koszul–Sidon Rigidity for (n+3) Cubic Decompositions over Dyadic RingsDOI 10.5281/zenodo.22850842
